## Details

Proteins with little detectable sequence similarity can still share a three-dimensional fold because structure is often more conserved than sequence over evolutionary time. Searching by structure can therefore reveal remote relationships, suggest possible functions, and identify structural neighbours that sequence searches may miss.

Structure search compares a query protein's three-dimensional model with structures in a database. Methods may superimpose atomic coordinates and measure geometric similarity, compare patterns of distances between residues, or encode local structural features as descriptors that can be searched efficiently at large scale. For example, Foldseek represents residue interactions with a structural alphabet and uses sequence-search techniques to find structurally similar proteins. [Learn more about Foldseek](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-01773-0).

The module is being developed through a collaboration between members of the
Australian Structural Biology Computing community and the team from
[Protein Data Bank in Europe (PDBe)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pdbe/).
